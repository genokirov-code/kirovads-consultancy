The real problem

Why most ad accounts underperform

Google and Meta make it easy to start spending. They don't make it easy to know what's working.

✕
Tracking that counts the wrong things Duplicate conversions, missing events, or a pixel that stopped firing months ago. The algorithm learns from these signals — if they're wrong, it optimises for the wrong people.
✕
Campaigns without a plan Boosted posts, a few keywords, settings left on default. Every euro needs a clear job: reach new people, bring them back, or close the sale.
✕
Following platform recommendations blindly Google and Meta's suggestions are built to increase spend. Some are useful. Many aren't right for your business.

What fixing it looks like

✓
Tracking you can trust GTM, GA4, Meta Pixel, Conversions API and Consent Mode v2 — set up and tested, so every real conversion is counted once.
✓
A structure built around your margins Campaigns organised by what makes you money, with clear targets for cost per lead or return on ad spend.
✓
Decisions based on your data I use AI to sort through campaign and tracking data quickly, then verify what it finds in your actual accounts. You only get recommendations I'd stand behind.
How I work

AI does the digging. A human makes the call.

AI tools are good at going through large amounts of data quickly. They're also capable of being confidently wrong. So I use them for what they're good at — and check everything by hand.

Step 1
🤖

AI-assisted analysis

Campaign exports, search terms, audience data and tracking setups are analysed with AI tools to spot patterns, anomalies and likely waste faster than a manual review alone.

Step 2
🔎

Human verification

I check every finding in your real accounts — Google Ads, Meta Events Manager, GA4, GTM. False alarms are removed. What's left is ranked by impact on your business.

Step 3
📋

A clear action plan

You get a short, prioritised list: what to fix first, why it matters, and what it should change. In plain language, not platform jargon.

AI saves time on analysis. It never makes the final decision about your budget.
